# Gacha Simulation: Pity Mechanics and Probability Psychology

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's a numerical adjustment to increase drop rates; in reality, the core mechanism uses "hard pity as a safety net" and "soft pity for smoothing" to eliminate extreme tail pain; this article focuses on how to verify the distribution via simulation and design emotional thresholds.

**Traffic Reason:** High controversy naturally leads to high comments. Everyone will ask: **"Is this probability actually a trap?"**

## 1) Relying Solely on "2% Chance" Will Fail

Players aren't broken by the "average probability," but by **consecutive failures**.

Therefore, a gacha system usually needs to solve two things:

- **Extreme non-luck tail probability** (Should P99 be outrageous?)
- **The feeling of being stabbed in the back** (Got a drop, but not the one you wanted)

## 2) The Most Common Combination: Hard Pity + Soft Pity + Directed

- **Hard Pity:** Guaranteed rare item after N pulls (baseline fairness)
- **Soft Pity:** Probability increases as you get closer to N (better perceived experience)
- **Directed / Big Pity:** If you pull the wrong one this time, you get it next (reduces negative emotions)

**Experience:** The improvement in reputation from soft pity is often much more noticeable than simply increasing the base probability.

## 3) Simulation Must Look at Distribution, Not Just Expectation

What you need isn't "average 50 pulls to get an item," but rather:

- P50: How many pulls half the players need
- P90: How many pulls most players need
- P99: How many pulls extreme non-luck players need (this decides whether public opinion explodes)

**One-sentence summary:** **Gacha balance is about "reducing tail pain," not "increasing the mean."**

---

## 4) How to Implement Soft Pity (Smoother Perceived Experience)

The most common implementation of soft pity isn't "suddenly doubling," but rather a gradually rising probability curve, for example:

- Pulls 1~A: Base probability `p0`
- Pulls A~N: Probability increases by `dp` per pull (or rises in segments/exponentially)
- Pull N: Hard pity guarantees a drop

What you need to tune is the "player experience," not mathematical elegance:

- Raising too early: Makes the base probability look like a prop
- Raising too late: The tail remains painful (reputation risk)

---

## 5) Directed / Big Pity: Solving "Got a Drop, But Not the One You Wanted"

Many controversies aren't about "not getting a rare," but about "got a drop, but it was the wrong one."

Common directed schemes:

- **50/50:** If you pull the wrong one this time, you get it next (big pity)
- **Counter:** Pulling the wrong one adds 1 point; once full, you get it
- **Redemption:** Exchange duplicate rares for "fragments," accumulating them to swap for the target

**Core Goal:** **Turn "bad luck" into accumulative progress.**

---

## 6) Using Monte Carlo to View Distribution (Minimum Executable Steps)

You don't need to do complex mathematical derivation first; simulation can reveal most problems immediately:

1. Write a `simulateOneUser()` that returns "number of pulls needed to get the target"
2. Run it `100000` times to get the distribution
3. Check `P50/P90/P95/P99`, and then look at the distribution of "how many times you pulled the wrong one"

You will find: Some configurations might have a great mean, but P99 is extremely ugly—this is the "reputation explosion point."

---

## 7) Review and Reputation Considerations (Product Side)

- **Consistent Disclosure Wording:** When displaying base probability, also explain pity and directed rules (otherwise players feel misled)
- **Avoid "Implicit Weighting" That Causes Unexplainability:** Players fear most feeling "controlled by the system"
- **Turn "Bad Luck" into Progress:** Even fragment redemption can significantly reduce negative emotions

## Boundary Conditions

- Need to handle whether extreme non-luck tail probability (P99) is outrageous enough to cause reputation explosion.
- Need to handle the feeling of being stabbed in the back caused by consecutive failures (got a drop, but not the one you wanted).
- Need to handle the impact of soft pity probability curve rising too early or too late on perceived experience.
- Need to handle whether "wrong pull" situations in directed mechanisms are converted into accumulative progress.
- Need to handle whether disclosure wording and implicit weighting rules are consistent, to avoid players feeling controlled.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit:** Soft pity improves reputation. **Cost:** Base probability looks like a prop. **Alternative:** Adjust the rising curve to avoid rising too early or too late.
- **Benefit:** Turns bad luck into accumulative progress (e.g., fragment redemption). **Cost:** Increases system complexity. **Alternative:** Directly increase base probability or rely solely on hard pity.
