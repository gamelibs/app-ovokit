# 2048 Variant: Players Aren't Angry at "Randomness," They're Angry at "Lose-Win States"

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's random number generation; in reality, the core mechanism is controlling board space and merge cancellation rates to adjust failure probability. This article focuses on explaining how to achieve "soft-controlled difficulty" that "looks random" via "Shuffle Bags" or "Weight Fine-Tuning."

Traffic Reason: Extremely strong classic gameplay cognition; players understand it at a glance and are willing to challenge "how far I can go."

## 1) Failure Probability Control: What You Actually Need to Control is "Space," Not "Whether to Spawn 2 or 4"

Player experience breakdowns usually occur in two scenarios:

- The board is filled but **few mergeable pairs exist** (waiting for death at every step)  
- Consecutive spawns cause a sudden spike in entropy (suddenly becoming unsolvable)

Therefore, the core strategy for controlling difficulty is:  
**Keep the situation maintained with "1~2 visible winning moves,"** making players feel "I can survive if I just play better."

## 2) Anti-Cheat: How to Make It "Look Random"

If you directly "give the player what you want," they'll feel manipulated; if you go fully random, the experience can plummet.

Two very useful intermediate solutions:

### A. Shuffle Bag (Shuffle Bag)
Pre-load the "numbers to appear in the next N turns" into a bag before drawing:  
Players see a random sequence, while the system sees **quota-controlled randomness**.

### B. State Weight Fine-Tuning (Only Fine-Tuning)
Assign a slight weight to "spawn 2/4/obstacles" based on the current state:

- Board nearly full: Increase low values (give merge space)  
- Merge chain too smooth: Increase high values or obstacles (avoid mindless snowballing)

Key Point: **Only make small, long-term tilts**, do not make short-term, counter-intuitive hard controls (e.g., player clearly needs a 2 but suddenly gets consecutive 4s).

## 3) A Very Practical Experience Metric

Use "Average Playable Steps / Move Count" as a rough metric:  
You will intuitively see if pure randomness leads to "early death," and also see if soft control makes the situation "too smooth" lacking challenge.

## Boundary Conditions

Issues that need handling:
- How to avoid players "waiting for death at every step" when the board is filled but few mergeable pairs exist.
- How to intervene in time when consecutive spawns cause a sudden entropy spike leading to unsolvability.
- How to maintain hope by keeping "1~2 visible winning moves" when the player is in a "lose-win state."
- How to ensure only small, long-term tilts are made when the system adjusts weights, avoiding short-term counter-intuitive operations.
- How to prevent the system from suddenly spawning high values (e.g., 4) when the player clearly needs a specific number (e.g., 2), thereby ruining the experience.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit:** Players feel "it looks random"; **Cost:** Pure randomness can lead to a plummeting experience; **Alternative:** Use Shuffle Bags or State Weight Fine-Tuning as intermediate solutions.
- **Benefit:** Maintaining "1~2 visible winning moves" by controlling space; **Cost:** The system needs to predict and intervene in the situation's trajectory; **Alternative:** Accept the high failure rate or overly smooth situation brought by pure randomness.
- **Benefit:** Using "Average Playable Steps / Move Count" as an intuitive metric; **Cost:** This metric is only a rough indicator; **Alternative:** None (no other alternative metrics provided in the original text).

One-sentence summary: **Players accept randomness, but not inevitable defeat; the best control is to make randomness "fluctuate like a human."**
