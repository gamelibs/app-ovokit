# Random Events: Balancing Good/Bad Ratios and Correcting Negative Feedback Loops

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's random event selection; in reality, the core mechanism is emotional management and pacing control via a "Pool + Weight + Constraint" model. This article focuses on how to avoid "malicious randomness" through ratio allocation, carry-over correction, and story consistency.

**Why it drives traffic:** It's unpredictable, and players love to discuss "what happened in this run." However, if the event system is done poorly, players will be scolded for "malicious randomness."

## 1) Good/Bad Ratios: Don't Go for 50/50

Events are not gacha pulls; events are about pacing.

A more stable ratio reference:

- 60% Neutral (provides information/route changes/small rewards)  
- 25% Good Events (speed boosts, healing, strong items)  
- 15% Bad Events (costs exchanged for larger rewards)

The baseline for bad events: **Make players feel "I made a wrong choice," not "The system is trolling me."**

## 2) Anti-Carry-Over: Must Correct Continuous Negative Feedback

What hurts players most isn't a single failure, but:

> A few consecutive bad events → Resources run dry → Forced into a dead end → Churn

Therefore, we recommend two layers of protection:

- **Soft Pity:** The longer the bad streak, the higher the weight of good events.  
- **Hard Pity:** Critical resources (healing/repair) must appear at least once every X attempts.

## 3) Make Randomness Feel Like a "Reasonable Story"

Event experience = Randomness + Consistency:

- Split event pools by chapter/biome (ice biome events differ from desert events)  
- Cool down on recent N event types (avoid repetition)  
- Events must offer at least two options (turn randomness into "choice")

One-sentence summary: **The essence of the event system is "emotional management," not "rolling dice."**

---

## 4) Event Weights: Use "Pool + Weight + Constraint" Instead of Pure Randomness

A deployable event selection model:

- Split event pools by chapter/biome (pool)
- Assign a base weight to each event (weight)
- Overlay constraints (constraints)

Typical examples of constraints:

- Cool down: After a similar event appears, it won't appear again within X attempts.
- Mutually exclusive: After one event appears, another is disabled.
- Prerequisite: Related events won't appear if a certain system isn't unlocked.

This way, you maintain the sense of randomness while avoiding "absurd carry-over."

---

## 5) Minimal Implementation of Continuous Negative Feedback Correction (Pity)

You don't need a complex system for pity; a minimal implementation can significantly improve experience:

- Maintain a `badStreak` (count of consecutive bad events)
- When `badStreak` increases, boost the weight of good events or inject guaranteed rescue resources.
- Once a good event/critical resource appears, reset `badStreak`.

Note: The goal of pity is not to "make players lucky forever," but to prevent emotions from being completely crushed by a streak.

---

## 6) Events Must Be "Reviewable": Otherwise You Can't Balance Them

We recommend implementing minimal logging during development:

- Event sequence for this run (eventId + choice + result)
- Key resource curve (HP/Gold/Durability)

You'll quickly discover "which type of event breaks players most easily at what stage," giving you evidence to adjust weights and cool downs.

## Boundary Conditions

- Bad events must make players feel they "made a wrong choice" rather than "the system is trolling them"; ensure the logic of cost vs. reward is self-consistent.
- Must handle cases where continuous bad events drain resources or force players into a dead end; trigger soft pity or hard pity mechanisms.
- Must handle the issue of repeated events; use type cool downs or mutual exclusion constraints to prevent player experience collapse.
- Must handle the risk of missing critical resources (e.g., healing/repair); ensure they appear at least once every X attempts to prevent churn.
- Must handle balance tuning during development; record the event sequence and key resource curve for this run to enable post-mortem analysis.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit:** Maintains unpredictability and discussion heat from randomness; **Cost:** Pure randomness easily causes continuous negative feedback, leading to player emotional breakdown; **Alternative:** Adopt the "Pool + Weight + Constraint" model, introducing pity and cool down mechanisms while retaining randomness.
- **Benefit:** Splitting pools by chapter or biome enhances story consistency of events; **Cost:** Limits the purity of global random distribution; **Alternative:** Overlay a cool down on the most recent N event types on top of split pools, ensuring thematic coherence while avoiding event repetition.
- **Benefit:** Minimal pity implementation (maintaining bad event count and adjusting weights only) significantly improves experience; **Cost:** Cannot achieve perfect luck equalization; **Alternative:** Do not pursue "luck forever"; set the baseline goal as merely "avoiding emotional breakdown."
