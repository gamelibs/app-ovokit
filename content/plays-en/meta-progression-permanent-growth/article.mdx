# Failure is Growth: Permanent Growth Ratios and Preventing "Grinding Failures"

## One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's unlocking out-of-match numbers or content; actually, the core mechanism is a balance between "short-term feedback driving attempts" and "long-cycle goals driving persistence"; this article focuses on explaining how to split growth into resources/experience and milestones, and set ratios to prevent numerical collapse.

Traffic reason: Significantly reduces frustration, making players more willing to keep trying; it's also the most common retention pillar for "Roguelike/Semi-Roguelike" games.

## 1) Permanent Growth Ratios: Too Strong Ruins the Game, Too Weak Ruins Motivation

Permanent growth should be split into three lines:

- **Numbers** (Minor)  
- **Features** (Unlock gameplay abilities)  
- **Content** (New weapons, new levels, new events)  

Experience range: Permanent numbers should account for only **20~35%** of total strength, leaving the rest for players to get stronger through understanding and building.

## 2) Preventing "Grinding Failures": Make the Optimal Strategy Not to Feed the Enemy

Common anti-grind measures:

- Tie rewards to performance (floors/levels, kills, task completion)  
- Early-exit decay (leaving before 2 minutes yields very low rewards)  
- Daily/match caps + milestone bonuses (encourages going further)  

## 3) Failure Settlement Must Give Clear Feedback

Players are willing to continue depending on whether the settlement can answer two questions:

1. What did I progress in this run?  
2. What changes will happen in the next run?  

One-sentence summary: **The core of "Failure is Growth" is "giving hope," but hope cannot come from "feeding."**

---

## 4) How to Split Permanent Growth (A Practical Structure)

Splitting out-of-match growth into two layers is more stable:

- **Short-cycle growth**: Progress in every run (resources/experience), making players willing to start another run immediately  
- **Long-cycle growth**: Milestone-based unlocks (new systems/new content), giving players a long-term goal  

This allows you to satisfy both "short-term feedback" and "long-term hope," instead of stacking all growth into numbers.

---

## 5) Three Strikes Against Grinding Failures (More Detailed)

### A. Strongly Bind Settlement to Progress

Do not allow "lying flat for 30 seconds to surrender" to get nearly full rewards. Bind instead:

- Reaching floor/level  
- Defeating elites/bosses  
- Completing in-run tasks (saving people, lighting altars, escorting, etc.)  

### B. Early-Exit Decay + Failure Decay

Two common decay mechanisms:

- **Early-exit decay**: For example, leaving within 2 minutes yields very low rewards  
- **Failure decay**: Rewards start decreasing after 3 consecutive failures on the same floor (forcing players to break through)  

### C. Bind "High Rewards" to "Correct Gameplay"

For example:

- Clearing with a specific weapon/specific build grants extra resources  
- Completing challenge conditions (no-hit, time limit, limited card count) grants extra resources  

Core purpose: Let players learn to play correctly for the rewards, not slouch for the rewards.

---

## 6) Acceptance Checklist (Check Before Launch)

- [ ] Can new players feel significantly stronger within 3~6 runs (must be perceptible)  
- [ ] Is intentional failure/early-exit more profitable than trying to progress (must NOT be)  
- [ ] Does mid-to-late game show "numerical domination" making gameplay meaningless (must AVOID)  

## Boundary Conditions

- New players must feel significantly stronger within 3~6 runs, otherwise the experience will feel broken.  
- The reward for intentional failure or early-exit must be lower than the reward for trying to progress, preventing players from slouching.  
- Mid-to-late game must avoid "numerical domination" making gameplay meaningless, preserving strategic space.  
- Settlement feedback must clearly answer the two questions: "What did I progress in this run?" and "What changes will happen in the next run?"  
- After 3 consecutive failures on the same floor, a reward decay mechanism must be triggered, forcing players to break through bottlenecks.  

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Short-term feedback (progress in every run) and long-term hope (milestone-based unlocks) coexist, improving retention and motivation; **Cost**: Cannot stack all growth into numbers, otherwise it's too strong and ruins the game.  
- **Benefit**: Rewards are strongly bound to performance (floors/levels/kills/tasks), making players learn correct gameplay for rewards; **Cost**: If binding is improper (e.g., allowing lying flat to get full rewards), it leads to players slouching for rewards.  
- **Benefit**: Both early-exit and failure have decay mechanisms to prevent grinding failures; **Cost**: Decay intensity needs fine control to avoid excessive frustration leading to player churn.
