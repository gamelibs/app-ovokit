# Pacman City: Turning "Dodge the Ghosts" into Timed Route Planning

## The Essence in One Sentence

Pacman City looks like a classic Pac-Man clone, but it actually reshapes the dodge-and-avoid archetype into a timed score-attack problem: within 120 seconds, the player constantly trades off "push progress by eating dots" against "stay alive by dodging ghosts" — and the power pellet's 8-second role reversal is the only rhythm switch in the game. When to take it, and where to hunt afterwards, decides the score ceiling.

## Behavior Loop

1. **Observe**: 323 dots cover the maze at the start; 4 ghosts (red / cyan / pink / orange) patrol the streets; the HUD shows score, lives and the countdown in real time.
2. **Plan a route**: pick a line that chains dots without running into a ghost's patrol path — this is where the game's puzzle flavor comes from.
3. **Execute**: steer Pacman with the arrow keys or the on-screen joystick at bottom-left; dots are eaten automatically on contact, +10 each.
4. **Power reversal**: eat a golden power pellet in a corner for +50 and 8 seconds of power mode, during which ghosts can be eaten for +200 each — the highest single score in the game.
5. **Back to dodging**: when power mode ends, avoidance resumes; colliding with a ghost in normal mode costs 1 of 3 lives.
6. **Settle and restart**: when the 120-second countdown hits zero or lives run out, the result screen offers "restart" (a full data reset) or "back to menu".

## Primitive Annotations

- `SetDirection(dx, dy)` – joystick / arrow-key input, converted into Pacman's heading
- `EatDot(+10)` – eat a dot on contact, add score, and check level completion
- `EnterPowerMode(8s)` – eat a power pellet: roles reverse, +50 points
- `EatGhost(+200)` – collide with a ghost during power mode for the top single score
- `CollideGhost()` – collide with a ghost in normal mode: lose 1 life and reset position
- `Tick(-1s)` – the countdown fires every second; reaching zero ends the run
- `Reset(score/lives/time)` – restart restores every value to its initial state

## Design Trade-offs

1. **A 120-second hard limit instead of "until the maze is clean"**: in a pure eat-everything mode, pressure comes only from ghosts. A hard countdown makes "greed" and "survival" mutually exclusive at every moment, compressing the dodge archetype into score attack — and a short run makes the result screen's "restart" almost free to tap.
2. **An 8-second power window**: the reversal is deliberately short. Inside the window, players charge at the very ghosts they were fleeing seconds ago; emotion swings from tension to release and back — the strongest flow oscillation this game has. A longer window would dilute it.
3. **A 10 / 50 / 200 score gradient**: reward is strictly aligned with risk. Dots are steady income, the pellet is the rhythm switch, and eating ghosts is high-risk high-reward — nudging players to plan routes like "swing by the pellet, then cut the ghost off on the way back".
4. **Dual input: joystick + keyboard**: the virtual joystick serves touch screens, arrow keys serve desktop. One set of gameplay logic covers both device classes, with the input layer fully decoupled from gameplay.
5. **4 ghosts on the field**: just enough to form moving blockades on the maze's arterial streets without sealing every route — combined with 3 lives of tolerance, beginners survive while veterans stay pressured.

## Implementation Notes

- **Thin rules, thick modules**: the four states boot → menu → gameplay → result migrate on events (`boot.complete` / `game.start` / `game.over` / `game.restart` / `game.menu`); the rule layer only reads/writes data and forwards events, never touching rendering.
- **Core module `core/pacman-city`**: owns maze data, dot layout, ghost AI and collision detection, exposing four actions — `reset` / `setDirection` / `checkLevelComplete` / `handlePlayerDeath`; clearing all dots advances the level.
- **Scene-private renderer**: `__OV.Games.PacmanCity.Game` draws the city maze, Pacman, ghosts, dots and HUD in PIXI, takes over joystick and keyboard input, and emits `pacman.move` to the kernel.
- **Timing sinks into the rule chain**: `game.tick` fires every second; a rule writes `remaining` into `timeRemaining`, and at ≤0 it sets `isGameActive=false` and emits `game.over`, which the result screen renders.
- **Zero custom UI code**: button binding on the menu and result screens is handled by the generic `common-ui` module, and the boot scene auto-advances to the menu.
