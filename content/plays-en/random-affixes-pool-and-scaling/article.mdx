# Equipment Randomized Affixes: Designing the Affix Pool and Preventing Numeric Collapse

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's random equipment drops; actually, the core mechanism uses grouped mutual exclusion and multiplicative stacking control to constrain numeric ceilings; this article focuses on explaining how to design the affix pool structure to balance rewards while maintaining the gaming experience.

Traffic reason: The gambling mechanic is naturally "talk-worthy"; players love posting screenshots and arguing about "lucky/unlucky," leading to high comment interaction.

## 1) The Affix Pool Must Be "Split Correctly" Before Discussing Probabilities

The most common pitfall: stuffing a bunch of affixes into one big pool and then relying on weights to balance.

A more reliable structure:

- **Split by body part**: Head/Chest/Boots/Weapon/Accessory  
- **Split by weapon type**: Melee/Range/Staff/Throwing  
- **Split by affix group**: Damage Group/Survival Group/Utility Group/Economy Group  

And set limits for each piece of equipment:

- Maximum 1~2 affixes from the same group can appear on a single item  
- Mutually exclude key multiplicative affixes (to avoid combination explosion)  
- Rarity determines "number of affixes + ceiling"  

## 2) The Core of Anti-Collapse: Control Stacking, Not Individual Values

Affix collapse usually stems from stacking chains:

> Crit Rate × Crit Damage × Multi-Hit × Trigger Chance × Extra Damage…

Recommended rules:

- **Same-group addition**: Try to stack within the same dimension (e.g., Crit Damage) using addition.  
- **Cross-group multiplication**: Multiplication is only allowed across different dimensions, but must be controlled for ceiling and mutual exclusion.  
- **Soft ceiling**: Diminishing returns feel more "random" than hard cuts.  

## 3) The Gambling Experience Must Be "Story-Worthy"

Players are willing to farm repeatedly or reroll repeatedly, provided that:

- I know what's in this affix pool (at least the categories and approximate probabilities)  
- I can change the outcome (reforge/lock affixes/tune)  
- I can finally show it off (affix names, effects, and numeric comparisons have a sense of ceremony)  

## Boundary Conditions

- Need to handle pool splitting across different body parts, weapon types, and affix groups, avoiding relying on weights to force-balance a big pool.
- Need to handle quantity limits for affixes within the same group (max 1~2 affixes) and the mutual exclusion logic for key multiplicative affixes.
- Need to handle the specific determining role of rarity on "number of affixes" and "ceiling."
- Need to handle the reward inflation caused by cross-group multiplication, controlling diminishing returns via a soft ceiling mechanism.
- Need to handle player demands for probability transparency, the ability to change results via reforging/tuning, and the sense of ceremony in finally showing off the results.  

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Splitting by body part, weapon type, and affix group ensures clear structure and reliable balance; mutually excluding key multiplicative affixes avoids combination explosion.
- **Cost**: Must give up the simple and crude solution of "stuffing a bunch of affixes into one big pool and balancing via weights."
- **Alternative**: Use "same-group addition, cross-group multiplication" stacking rules instead of directly controlling individual values, and use "soft ceiling" instead of "hard cuts" to simulate randomness.  

One-sentence summary: **Randomized affixes are not gambling; they are "controllable pursuit of better."**
