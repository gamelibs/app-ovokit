# Infinite Progression: The Exponential Cost Curve and "Fake Infinite" Design

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's a progression process where numbers keep rising; in reality, the core mechanism involves artificially creating a sense of "stages" by adjusting the cost growth rate in segments. This article focuses on explaining how to design a "fake infinite" loop by combining exponential curves with reward limits.

**Traffic Driver:** Extremely satisfying numerical feedback, low barrier to entry (the "numbers skyrocketing" effect is very intuitive in short videos).

## 1) The Cost Curve Determines How Long You Can "Enjoy"

The default solution for infinite progression is exponential:

> cost = base × r^level

But the real trick is **adjusting r in segments**:

- Early game: small r (satisfying, retains players)
- Mid game: medium r (maintains a sense of goal)
- Late game: large r (extends lifespan, prevents beating the game in a day)

Note: Do not let rewards grow exponentially along with costs, or the system will crash even faster.

## 2) "Fake Infinite" Is Easier to Content-Design Than True Infinite

Break the infinite goal into stages:

- Reach milestones → unlock new systems (automation, rebirth, new resources)
- Use rebirth to reset growth faster (players are willing to repeat the grind)
- Use seasons/rankings to turn "infinite" into a short, comparable cycle

## 3) Players Can Tolerate Waiting, But Not "No Change"

Experience thresholds:

- At least one visible improvement every 30~90 seconds (numbers/effects/unlocks)
- Large number displays need a unit system (K/M/B/… or scientific notation)

One-sentence summary: **The essence of infinite progression is a "sense of stages," not "no end."**

---

## 4) Reward Curves Should Not Match the Same Exponent as Costs (Otherwise It Crashes in a Day)

Many "true infinite" failures stem from cost growing exponentially and rewards growing exponentially as well, resulting in:

- Instant astronomical numbers in the late game (overflow/precision issues)
- Gameplay being crushed by numbers (strategy becomes irrelevant)

A more stable combination:

- Cost: exponential / segmented exponential
- Reward: sub-linear (logarithmic / square root / segmented linear)

From an engineering perspective, you can apply a simple cap first:

- Ensure the reward growth rate is always lower than the cost growth rate
- Or implement soft caps (diminishing returns)

---

## 5) How to Make "Sense of Stages" Configurable

A common staged strategy:

- Trigger a milestone every X levels
  - Unlock new resources/new systems (automation, rebirth, research)
  - Replace some upgrades (old upgrades retire, new ones appear)

You can make stages into a table:

- `phaseId`
- `levelRange`
- `costMultiplier`
- `rewardMultiplier`
- `unlock` (new feature toggle)

This way, you don't need to write "if level > 1000 ..." everywhere in the code.

---

## 6) Display and Storage: Large Numbers Are an Engineering Problem, Not Just a UI Detail

When numbers start getting big, you must at least consider:

- Display unit system: `k/m/b` or scientific notation (keep it consistent)
- Storage precision: `number` will distort around 2^53
- Rankings/Comparisons: need a serializable, comparable representation

If you don't implement "true infinite" in the MVP phase, it is recommended to trigger rebirth/settlement once a certain threshold is reached to avoid falling into the large number trap.

## Boundary Conditions

Issues that need handling:
- If reward growth rate matches the exponential cost growth, it leads to instant astronomical numbers in the late game, causing overflow or precision distortion.
- If players cannot obtain visible improvements for a long time (e.g., exceeding 90 seconds), it creates frustration leading to churn.
- If large number displays lack a unit system (K/M/B) or scientific notation, it causes player cognitive confusion.
- If stored numbers exceed a specific threshold (e.g., 2^53), precision loss occurs, rendering ranking comparisons ineffective.
- If the numerical system lacks stage thresholds (e.g., rebirth/settlement) for resetting, players will lose motivation due to an infinite goal.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Reward / Cost**: Rewards use sub-linear growth (logarithmic/square root/segmented linear), while costs use exponential or segmented exponential growth, ensuring gameplay strategy space is not crushed by numbers.
- **Substitution**: Use a "sense of stages" (milestones unlocking new systems, rebirth resetting) to substitute the endless loop of "true infinite," solving issues of rapid content consumption and numerical collapse.
