# Roguelike Slaying: Enemy Density Formulas and Screen Readability

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's about piling up enemy counts; in reality, the core mechanism is controlling "Threat Value" budgets and pacing in segments. This article focuses on explaining how to guarantee screen readability through hard rules.

**Why it goes viral:** High tension + extreme satisfaction; "screen explosions" in short videos easily go viral.

## 1) Enemy Density Isn't "More is Better," It's a Triplet: Count × Speed × Size

The difficulty of slaying games feels more like "information pressure" rather than "damage pressure."

If you increase all three simultaneously:

- Count (more on screen)  
- Speed (closer contact faster)  
- Size (harder to dodge)  

Players will immediately lose a sense of control.

A more stable approach: Use a **spawn budget** to control difficulty—treat the "Threat Value" that can be spawned per second as the budget:

- Minions: Low threat value, responsible for filling space  
- Elites: High threat value, responsible for turning points  
- Ranged enemies: Threat value rises with the number of screen projectiles  

## 2) The Density Curve Should Be "Segmented"

A common 10-minute pacing:

- **0~1 minute**: Rapid heat-up (let players feel satisfied immediately)  
- **1~4 minutes**: Steady growth (let players feel they can control it)  
- **After 4 minutes**: Explosive peak (create memorable moments via elites/events)  

If you use linear growth throughout, two problems arise:

1. Early game is too bland, causing new players to churn out  
2. Late game explodes too early, breaking the experience  

## 3) Readability: Must Be Written as Hard Rules (Otherwise You'll Just Cut Effects at the End)

Recommended to hard-code three red lines:

1. **Max Projectile/Effect Count**: If exceeded, "downsample/merge visuals."  
2. **Player Silhouette Priority**: Outlines, high contrast, keep center blank—always ensure the player is visible.  
3. **Danger Information Priority**: Damage zones > Dash trails > Enemy bodies > Environmental decorations.  

If readability is done right, you can even make the numbers harsher, because players will feel they "die with understanding."

## Edge Cases

- Need to handle invalid input causing instant player death.
- Need to handle repeated operations causing infinite enemy respawning.
- Need to handle whether to reset state immediately after game end.
- Need to handle how to judge win/loss in a tie or settlement.
- Need to handle resource or data anomalies causing out-of-bounds values.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Creates tension and satisfaction via segmented curves; **Cost**: Linear growth throughout leads to bland early game or collapsed late game; **Alternative**: Use spawn budget to control difficulty.
- **Benefit**: Hard-code three red lines to guarantee readability; **Cost**: May limit effect richness; **Alternative**: Just cut effects at the end.

One-sentence summary: **The difficulty of slaying isn't "making explosions," it's "making explosions still readable."**
