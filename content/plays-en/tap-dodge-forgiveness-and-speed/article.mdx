# Click Dodge: Tension via "Pressure", Retention via "Forgiveness"

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it increases difficulty; actually, the core is using "edge-case"判定 and "near-miss feedback" to transform failure into a highlight; this article focuses on how to design explainable forgiveness and near-miss mechanisms.

Traffic reason: High tension, very watchable on short videos; viewers can immediately understand "barely missed death."

## 1) Forgiveness in Failure Judgment: Make Players Feel "I Made a Mistake", Not "The System Screwed Me"

Forgiveness doesn't make the game easier; it makes failure more explainable:

- **Visuals vs. Judgment Separation**: Bullets look like they grazed, but the judgment doesn't kill (players feel strong).
- **Near-Miss Feedback**: Slow-mo/sound effects/glows when grazing, turning "barely missed" into a highlight.
- **Respawn Protection**: Invincibility for 0.5~1s after revival, avoiding frustration from consecutive deaths.

A common pitfall: Making judgment too precise (like competitive games); casual players will just feel "this is unfair."

## 2) Bullet Speed Curve: Teach Them to Live Early, Make Them Steady Mid, Let Them Show Off Late

Suggested breakdown of one match into three segments:

1. **First 10s: Rapid Ramp-up** (build tension)
2. **Mid-game: Plateau** (let players practice stability)
3. **Late-game: Short Burst** (create highlights and shareability)

If you linearly accelerate from 0, the early game is boring; if you start fast, newbies can't learn.

## 3) One Experience Goal You Can Use Directly

Set the goal for "first playthrough" as:
**Players die once, live once, die again within 20~40s.**
This satisfies both "tension" and "learnability."

One-sentence summary: **The highlight of click dodge is "barely missed," so you must systematically engineer near-misses, not drive players away with precise judgment.**

---

## 4) Near-Miss is Designable, Not Luck

If you want players to feel "barely missed," you need to make near-miss an explicit mechanic:

- Near-miss zone: Add a "grazing zone" ring outside the judgment circle
- Trigger condition: Bullet enters grazing zone but doesn't hit
- Feedback: Slow-mo 100~200ms / slight screen shake / sound effect / score bonus

This turns "barely failed" from frustration into a highlight, greatly boosting virality and replayability.

---

## 5) Bullet Spawning: Create Pressure via "Density", Not Just "Speed"

Relying only on acceleration easily leads to two problems:

- Late game becomes unplayable (players only have muscle memory)
- Early game is boring (too slow before acceleration)

A more stable approach is to split pressure into two dimensions:

- Speed
- Density (bullets per unit time / coverage)

Common curves:

- Speed: Rapid ramp-up early → plateau mid → burst late
- Density: Gradually increase, but leave "breathing room" (space for player movement)

---

## 6) Acceptance Goals (Metrics for First Playthrough)

Setting measurable experience metrics makes goals more stable:

- Newbie first match average survival `20~40s`
- At least one near-miss feedback within `60~90s` (players feel "I'm strong")
- Respawn protection avoids immediate quit due to "consecutive deaths"

## Boundary Conditions

- No near-miss feedback triggered if bullet grazes outside the judgment circle but doesn't enter the "grazing zone."
- Ignore all bullet collision judgment if player is in respawn protection period (0.5~1s invincible).
- If bullet spawning density is too high leaving no movement space, adjust spawn rhythm or reduce quantity to leave "breathing room."
- If newbie first playthrough doesn't trigger near-miss feedback within 60~90s, check if speed curve is too flat.
- If player dies consecutively more than a certain count (e.g., 2 times), force end current match or extend respawn protection time to prevent frustration.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Near-miss feedback makes players feel "barely missed death," boosting satisfaction and virality; **Cost**: If judgment is too precise, casual players feel "the system screwed me" and churn; **Alternative**: Simulate grazing via visuals/sound/slow-mo instead of modifying collision radius.
- **Benefit**: Speed curve uses "ramp-up - plateau - burst" three-segment structure, balancing tutorial, stability, and highlight moments; **Cost**: Pure linear acceleration leads to boring early game or unplayable late game; **Alternative**: Split pressure into "speed" and "density" independently controlled, with reserved breathing room.
- **Benefit**: Respawn protection period (0.5~1s invincible) avoids frustration from consecutive deaths; **Cost**: Extending invincibility time may reduce tension; **Alternative**: Maintain pressure by increasing bullet spawning density or adjusting speed curve, rather than simply extending invincibility time.
