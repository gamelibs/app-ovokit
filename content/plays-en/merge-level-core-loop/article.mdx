# Core Logic of Merge & Upgrade Gameplay

Merge & Upgrade gameplay might look simple: combining two identical items to create a higher-tier one.  
However, its addictive nature relies not on the merge rules themselves, but on **output pacing, feedback intensity, and bottleneck design**.

This article breaks down the core of Merge into "modules you can reuse directly" and provides more robust engineering implementation approaches (table-driven, event logs, replayable).

---

## 1. The Essence of Merge: Turning "Repetitive Actions" into "Visible Growth"

What players repeatedly do is essentially one thing: **making the situation better**.

Therefore, the design goal is not to "keep players merging," but to ensure players have clear, visible rewards at every stage:

- Merging produces "stronger units" or "higher output"
- Unlocking new units creates "new objectives"
- Bottlenecks motivate players with the feeling of "I'm just one step away from passing"

---

## 2. The Minimal Core Loop (Closed Loop)

A functional minimal closed loop:

1. Produce resources/units (automatically or manually)
2. Player chooses placement and merging
3. Merging produces higher-tier units (bringing higher output or stronger abilities)
4. Unlock new units/grid cells/areas
5. Enter the next round with a higher goal

From an engineering perspective, it is recommended to decompose this loop into three types of events:

- `spawn` (production)
- `move/swap` (movement and layout)
- `merge` (merging and upgrading)

This makes implementing "undo/replay/recording" much easier later on.

---

## 3. Merge Rules: Must Be Table-Driven (Do Not Hardcode in Logic)

The most common pitfall: writing merge rules as `if (a.level===b.level) a.level++`.

Once you need to implement:

- Different branches (A merges to B, B merges to C, etc.)
- Special units (requiring 3-in-1 merges or cross-level merges)
- Costs (spending gold/stamina/materials)

Hardcoding will break quickly.

It is recommended to use a **merge table** to drive the logic:

- `from -> to`
- `cost`
- `fx` (effects/sound)
- `unlock` (whether new content is unlocked)

This allows designers and developers to collaborate on the same configuration file.

---

## 4. Output Pacing: Bury the "Highs" into the Curve, Rather Than Stacking Content

Merge retention largely comes from pacing design:

- Small rewards: Every merge provides feedback (sound effects, flashes, jumping numbers)
- Big rewards: Every N merges gives a strong feedback (new units, loot boxes, expansion, skills)

A simple, actionable approach:

- Drive strong feedback using a "merge counter" or "milestones"
- Use "rare drops/random events" for variety (but keep it controlled to avoid a feeling of gambling losses)

The key point: Strong feedback must be predictable (players know they are working towards it).

---

## 5. Bottlenecks (Gates): Don't Rely Solely on "Higher Numbers"; Let Players Make Choices

Common bottlenecks in Merge:

- Not enough grid space (space pressure)
- Output is too slow (time pressure)
- Key units are hard to merge (combination pressure)

A better bottleneck does not just force players to "grind," but guides them to make choices:

- Should you merge a high-tier unit that takes up space, or clear the field first?
- Should you expand the grid, or increase output first?
- Should you "sell/recycle" low-tier units for resources?

When players feel "I lost because I made the wrong choice," rather than "the system's numbers blocked me," the experience improves significantly.

---

## 6. Engineering Implementation: Use Event Logs to Ensure Replayability and Debuggability

If you convert all actions into events (`spawn`/`move`/`merge`), you gain:

- Bug reproduction: Record the event sequence to reproduce issues
- Undo/Redo: Roll back or recalculate based on events
- Replay/Recording: Play events chronologically

The minimal implementation can also be simple:

- Within a session: store `events[]`
- On each action: generate an event, update state
- For debugging: output events as JSON

---

## 7. Minimal Acceptance Checklist (Must Test Before Launch)

- [ ] Merge rules table: Every `from` can find a `to` (or is explicitly unmergeable)
- [ ] Space pressure: How to handle production when the grid is full (pause production/convert to reward/auto-merge?)
- [ ] Boundary between merge and move: Does dragging to an invalid position cause a bounce-back?
- [ ] Resource consistency: Gold/materials will not appear negative or double-deducted
- [ ] Save/Load: State is consistent after loading (grid, units, counters, milestones)

## Edge Cases

- Need to handle whether production pauses, converts to a reward, or triggers auto-merge when the grid is full
- Need to handle bounce-back or cancellation of operations when dragging to an invalid position
- Need to handle priority judgment when merge and move operations conflict
- Need to handle cases where resource deduction results in negatives or double deductions
- Need to handle whether counters, milestones, and other states are consistent after loading a save

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Flexibility and maintainability driven by the merge table; **Cost**: Increased initial configuration workload; **Alternative**: Hardcoded rules, but difficult to scale
- **Benefit**: Replayability, debuggability, and undo capabilities driven by event logs; **Cost**: Increased storage and computation overhead; **Alternative**: Store only keyframes or rely on in-memory state rollback
