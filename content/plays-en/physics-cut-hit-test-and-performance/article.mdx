# Physical Cutting: Stable Collisions Matter More Than "CAD-Level Precision"

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it simulates realistic geometric splitting; in reality, the core relies on a "coarse-to-fine" culling strategy and "bounded complexity" fragment rules to ensure stability. This article focuses on how to make hard trade-offs between collision stability and physical performance via configuration parameters.

**Why it works:** Strong physical feedback; players immediately feel "it cut, it shattered, it fell."

## 1) Collision Detection: Coarse-to-Fine for Stability

The biggest killer of cutting experience is:  
The player clearly swiped, but nothing happened (trust broken).

Common detection pipeline (recommended):

1. **Coarse cull (AABB/Circle):** Quickly exclude 90% of non-intersecting objects  
2. **Fine check (Segment vs Polygon):** Perform exact intersection only on candidates  
3. **Result handling:** Generate split polygons, apply physical impulse, play effects

If your budget is tight, prefer:

- Slightly looser collision detection  
- Slightly lower physical precision  
Over appearing to hit but getting no reaction.

## 2) Splitting Strategy: Satisfaction Comes from "It Looks Shattered," Not "Infinite Shards"

Engineering-wise, you must set an upper limit:

- Generate at most N pieces per cut (e.g., 6~10)  
- Fragments below the area threshold disappear directly or merge into particles  
- If polygon vertex count exceeds the threshold, simplify (to avoid physics calculation explosion)

## 3) Balancing Performance and Precision: Encode Budget as Rules

Recommend writing these into your config:

- `maxPiecesPerCut`  
- `minPieceArea`  
- `maxPolygonVertices`  
- `maxCutChecksPerFrame`

One-sentence summary: **The "satisfaction" of a cutting mechanic is built on stable detection and controllable performance; chasing extreme precision will ruin the feel.**

---

## 4) Segment vs Polygon: Minimal Viable Intersection Detection

When entering the "fine check" phase, the most common approach is:

- Form a line segment (or polyline) from the cutting trajectory  
- Detect if it intersects the target polygon edges

Key points for the minimal implementation:

- Allow some tolerance (epsilon) to avoid jitter when grazing the edge  
- Sample high-speed swipes: use a polyline segment instead of a single segment (otherwise it will tunnel through)

In terms of feel, it's better to "cut slightly loosely" than to "look like you swiped but nothing happened."

---

## 5) Split Generation: Enforce "Bounded Complexity" Shattering

The real difficulty isn't intersection detection, but ensuring the generated splits maintain:

- Polygon vertex count stays reasonable (otherwise physics explosion)  
- Tiny fragments are handled (otherwise jitter everywhere)

Practical rules:

- If generated area `< minPieceArea`: disappear directly or become particles  
- If vertex count `> maxPolygonVertices`: simplify (e.g., Douglas–Peucker) or degenerate to convex hull  
- Generate at most `maxPiecesPerCut` pieces per cut; merge or discard if exceeded

## 6) Physics Engine Collaboration: Don't Expect "Shards to Look Good Naturally"

Once splits are generated, to make them "look satisfying," you usually need to manually add a bit of:

- Impulse in the cutting direction, to fling shards apart  
- Slight angular velocity, to make shards spin  
- Tuned friction/damping for ground contacts, to avoid infinite jitter

These are all part of the "presentation layer"; don't treat them as cheating.

---

## 7) Acceptance Checklist (Stability First)

- [ ] High-speed swipes don't miss detection (sampling/polyline effective)  
- [ ] Continuous cutting doesn't let fragment count run out of control (upper limit enforced)  
- [ ] Tiny fragments don't cause a performance avalanche (area threshold effective)  
- [ ] Cutting edge jitter is acceptable (epsilon/simplification effective)

## Edge Cases

- Must handle high-speed swipes where single-segment detection tunnels through: use polyline sampling or tolerance (epsilon) as a fallback.  
- Must handle continuous cutting causing fragment count to run out of control: set a max pieces per cut limit and force merge or discard.  
- Must handle tiny-area fragments causing a performance avalanche: set a minimum area threshold and make them disappear or convert to particles.  
- Must handle excessive polygon vertex counts causing physics calculation explosion: set a vertex count limit and simplify or degenerate.  
- Must handle jitter when the cutting edge just grazes the target: introduce a tolerance mechanism to avoid "hit but no reaction."

## Design Trade-offs

- **Gain:** Slightly looser detection, slightly lower physical precision; **Cost:** Visual/physical presentation is less precise than CAD; **Alternative:** Use "looks shattered" bounded fragments and manual impulses instead of infinite-shard realistic physics.  
- **Gain:** Encode budget as rules (e.g., `maxPiecesPerCut`, `minPieceArea`); **Cost:** Cannot achieve theoretically perfect cutting effects; **Alternative:** Use config items to forcibly limit computation and generated object counts, prioritizing frame rate.
