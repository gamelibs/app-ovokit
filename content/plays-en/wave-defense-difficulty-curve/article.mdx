# Wave Defense: Wave Intensity Curves and Mid-Game Buffer Design

## The One-Sentence Essence

On the surface, it's random enemy swaps or pausing the game; actually, the core is using a "budget" to control total resources, creating strategic space through "peak-valley" rhythms and "consumable windows"; this article focuses on explaining how to quantify intensity with formulas and constrain variant frequencies to maintain the rhythm.

Traffic reason: Clear rhythm, strong visual impact ("Wave 10 is here!"), perfect for short video marathons.

## 1) Wave Intensity Must Be Managed via "Budget"

Don't just write "Wave N spawns 20 enemies"; instead, assign a budget per wave:

- Quantity
- Health Points (HP)
- Speed
- Special Enemies (flying/heavy armor/self-destruct/healing)

The benefit of a budget is: you can swap different templates to create variation without changing the overall difficulty.

## 2) Mid-Game Buffer: Give Players Time to Make Strategy

Tower defense fears "panic throughout the whole game" where no strategic space occurs.

A breathing period can provide:

- Build/upgrade windows
- Choose-one rewards (more money/heal/tower upgrade)
- Optional risk: advance the wave for extra rewards

With a buffer, players feel "win/loss is my choice," rather than "the system is spawning too fast."

## 3) Peaks and Valleys Must Exist

Stable growth doesn't mean every wave is harder; instead:

- 3~4 waves of stable growth
- 1 wave of peak challenge
- 1 wave of breathing reward

One-sentence summary: **The fun of wave defense comes from "predictable rhythm," not "pure stacking of enemies."**

---

## 4) The Minimum Formula for Intensity Curves (So You Can "Calculate" Instead of "Guess")

You need at least one explainable intensity metric to measure roughly how hard a wave is. A practical approximation is:

- `pressure = Σ (hp * speedWeight * abilityWeight)`

Where:

- `speedWeight`: The faster the speed, the harder it is to kite and focus fire (weight can be >1)
- `abilityWeight`: Abilities like flying/heavy armor/healing force players to switch strategies (weight is usually higher)

With this metric, you can avoid uncontrolled jumps like "Wave 9 is 300, Wave 10 suddenly 900."

---

## 5) How to Make the Mid-Game Buffer Feel Like a "Reward" Instead of a "Gap"

Common buffer practices:

- Stop spawning for 8~12 seconds (for upgrading/building)
- Continue spawning but reduce pressure (let players fight while adjusting)

To make the buffer feel more like a reward, suggest giving a clear "consumable window":

- Choose-one (more money/more tower/heal)
- Low-risk resource wave (weak enemies but lots of money)
- Optional risk: advance the wave for extra rewards

Key point: **Let players make visible "stronger choices" during the buffer period.**

---

## 6) Variant Appearance Frequency Must Be Controllable (Avoid Aesthetic Fatigue/Unsolvable)

If you treat variants as "random draws," it's easy to get:

- 4 consecutive flying waves (ground tower players crash immediately)
- 3 consecutive heavy armor waves (rhythm drags)

Suggest adding simple constraints:

- Cooldown: After a variant appears, it won't appear again within X waves
- Budget cap: Variant cost proportion must not exceed a certain threshold
- Fallback: If the player fails the previous wave, automatically lower the peak of the next wave or give a buffer reward

## Boundary Conditions

Issues that need handling:
- If the player encounters the same type of variant consecutively (e.g., consecutive flying or heavy armor), does it trigger a cooldown or lower intensity as a fallback.
- When the player fails the previous wave, does the next wave automatically lower the peak or give a buffer reward to avoid being unsolvable.
- Does the variant cost proportion exceed the preset threshold; if so, limit the generation of variants for that wave.
- During the mid-game buffer, if the player chooses to "advance the wave," does it synchronously adjust subsequent wave intensity or resources.
- When speed or ability weights in the intensity calculation are abnormal, does it cause the peak-valley rhythm to break.

## Design Trade-offs

- **Benefit**: Using budget instead of fixed quantity allows flexible template swapping to create variation; **Cost**: Increases calculation and management complexity; **Alternative**: Hardcode exactly how many enemies spawn in Wave N.
- **Benefit**: Mid-game buffer provides build/upgrade windows for player strategy choices; **Cost**: If mishandled, it becomes a mere empty gap period; **Alternative**: Continuous high-pressure spawning throughout but with no strategic space.
- **Benefit**: A clear consumable window (e.g., choose-one) makes the buffer period feel like a reward; **Cost**: Increases the burden of player decision-making; **Alternative**: Stop spawning for 8~12 seconds, letting players wait passively.
- **Benefit**: Quantifying intensity with formulas avoids uncontrolled jumps; **Cost**: Requires defining specific weights for speed and abilities; **Alternative**: Subjectively judge each wave's difficulty based on experience.
- **Benefit**: Constraining variant appearance frequency avoids aesthetic fatigue and unsolvable situations; **Cost**: Limits the surprise element of randomness; **Alternative**: Completely random variant drawing.
